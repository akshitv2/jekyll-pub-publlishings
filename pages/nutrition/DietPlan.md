## The Cumulative Nutrients (Perfect for Alternating)

- For these nutrients, your body has substantial storage capacity. Your average intake over a rolling 7-to-14-day window
  is what matters.
- Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K): Stored in the liver and fat tissues for months.
- Most Minerals (Iron, Calcium, Zinc, Magnesium): Stored in bones and organs.
- Vitamin B12: Uniquely among water-soluble vitamins, the liver stores enough B12 to last several years.

If you eat a massive serving of spinach and sweet potatoes on Tuesday, your body stores the Vitamin A and iron, allowing
you to safely coast through Thursday without eating any.

## The Consistent-Need Nutrients (Better Daily)

- These nutrients cannot be stored in large amounts. While alternating days won't cause a clinical deficiency, your body
  runs best when it gets a steady supply.
- Protein: The body has no storage form for amino acids (the building blocks of protein). Excess protein eaten on Monday
  cannot be saved for Wednesday's muscle repair; it is simply burned for energy or stored as body fat.
- Vitamin C and B-Vitamins (except B12): These water-soluble vitamins circulate in your fluids. Excess amounts are
  excreted in your urine within hours, meaning a massive dose on Monday won't help you on Wednesday.
- Water and Electrolytes (Sodium, Potassium): Filtered continuously by the kidneys; required daily to maintain fluid
  balance and nerve signaling.

## Eliminating Absorption Blockers (Phytates & Tannins)

- The Tea/Coffee Gap: Polyphenols and tannins in chai and coffee inhibit iron absorption. Avoid drinking tea or coffee
  for 1 hour before and 2 hours after your main meals.
